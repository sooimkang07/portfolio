Service design · Mobile · 2025

neuk is a mobile booking experience for private screening rooms, bringing the social experience of watching together back to streaming. From finding a room to unlocking the door and controlling the screen, everything happens in one app.

Role
Solo product designer
Timeline
6 weeks
October 2025
Platform
Mobile app
Concept
Scope
Service design
Product design
Branding
Strategy

Problem

Everyone wants to watch together. Nobody has the room.

In cities like New York, apartments are small and shared, and the third spaces that could hold a group cost money and fill up fast. Friends still want to gather for the finale, the premiere, or the big game, but every place they can go gives something up.

Opportunity

How might we create a shared viewing experience that combines the comfort of home, the quality of a theater, and the social energy of going out?

Solution

A private screening room, booked and controlled from your phone.

neuk connects room discovery, hourly booking, contactless entry, and streaming controls in one mobile experience.

Browse

Explore available screening rooms by location, time, and group size, with additional filters to find the right fit.

Book

Compare rooms, check availability, and reserve a private space by the hour.

Check in

Access your upcoming reservation and scan your QR ticket to enter the room.

Stream

Connect your phone to the room's screen and control playback directly from the app. Streaming services are already set up and signed in, so guests don't need to use their personal accounts.

Three room sizes, priced by the hour

Groups pick a size before they pick a room. The room size filter explains each tier up front: who it fits, what it costs per hour, and the minimum booking.

Core flows

From making plans to pressing play.

Four connected flows take users from discovering neuk to enjoying a private screening with friends.

GET STARTED

Make it easy to get started.

A quick onboarding introduces neuk and takes users straight into browsing spaces and exploring what's available.

Discover & Book

Find the right fit, then make it yours.

Search by location, time, and group size, then refine results with additional filters. Compare rooms in list or map view, explore available time slots, and book by the hour.

Check In

Make getting in as easy as showing up.

Upcoming bookings stay accessible in the app, with a reminder an hour before each reservation. On arrival, users pull up their booking and scan the QR code to enter.

Start Watching

Scan, connect, and start watching.

Scan the QR code on the room’s screen to pair your phone and turn the app into a remote for browsing streaming services, controlling playback, and tracking time remaining. Past and upcoming bookings stay accessible in the app for future visits.

High fidelity

One connected experience, from onboarding to playback.

The final screens bring discovery, booking, room access, and streaming controls into a consistent mobile interface.

Research

People don’t go out to watch a movie. They go to be together.

I started with the market, then talked to people. A competitive analysis showed the gap between the places people already watch together. Interviews showed what keeps them from doing it more.

Competitive analysis

Every option gives something up. None has it all.

I compared movie theaters, apartments, and public spaces like bars and watch parties on what they offer and where they fall short. Theaters have the screen, apartments have the comfort and control, and bars have the energy. Nothing offered all of it in a private room.

Screener

Groups outgrow living rooms, and they’d book a room to fix it.

Before the interviews, a screener confirmed who to talk to. Six people who watch with friends every week answered, and their answers made the case for a bookable room: the groups are bigger than the space, and everyone was interested.

User interviews

Small apartments, big groups, and a group chat that never decides.

A screener found six people who watch with friends regularly. I interviewed each one about how they watch, where, and what gets in the way, then sorted the notes into an affinity map.

01

A middle ground between home and theater

Home is comfortable but cramped and a chore to host. Theaters are immersive but rigid and impersonal.

02

Planning is the pain

Finding a time, getting everyone there, and splitting the cost kill the plan before it starts.

03

The people matter more than the movie

Time with friends is the draw, and many want a plan that isn’t built around drinking.

04

Worth it when it’s split

Price worried people until it was framed per person: a private theater night for about $20 each.

System thinking

Designing the app and the physical experience as one service

I translated research findings into personas, an app structure, and a service blueprint connecting what users do on their phones with what happens inside the screening room.

User Personas

Three viewing habits, three different priorities

Each persona combines two interviewees with similar habits and needs.

System map

Organizing the experience around the full booking journey

The app is organized around the booking loop: find a room, get the ticket, pair the remote. Food and drink got its own tab in the map, but I scoped it out of the first version.

Service blueprint

Connecting every digital touchpoint to the physical service behind it

The blueprint follows a group through five stages and lines up what they do, what they see in the app and the room, what staff do behind the scenes, and the systems underneath. Food and drink lives here as a staffed service, ready for the app when it’s designed.

Iterations & testing

People wanted cozy but elevated: the energy of going out with the comfort of home.

Before designing screens, I tested the vibe. That set up three visual directions, and a low-fidelity flow showed what the screens had to fix.

Concept testing

Participants sorted rooms and food into what was and wasn’t their vibe.

Like a card sort with pictures: each participant sorted rooms, food and drink setups, and app features into my vibe, could sometimes be my vibe, and not my vibe, and left comments on what drew them in.

01

Not extremes

People gravitated to a balance of social energy and homey comfort.

02

Cozy but elevated

Warm lighting and soft seating, with a screen worth leaving home for.

03

Not a club

Neon, party-like spaces felt too distracting or impersonal for most.

Design directions

Three directions, each pushing one extreme.

Lume leaned into the quality of a theater, neuk into the comfort of home, and Streamie into the energy of going out. The final design keeps the best of the first two.

Low-fidelity testing

Testers wanted to see more, be told more, and control less.

Eight testers left notes screen by screen, from room cards that were too small to confusion about what happens after booking. Drag or scroll the strip to read them.

Lainey

Bigger room cards

“Room cards are too small.” Cards grew, and more of each photo shows.

Paul

Guidance after booking

“I’m confused on the process after booking it.” A reminder, the ticket, and a welcome in the room now walk the group through.

Grace

A simpler remote

“Just on/off works for me.” The full color wheel for the lights became a simple toggle.

Branding

Cinematic enough for a night out, cozy enough to feel like home.

I started by deciding what neuk is and isn’t, ran a naming sprint, and sketched the mark by hand. The system takes Lume’s dark palette, blue accent, and thin lines, and neuk’s name, rounded shapes, and warm cream.

What neuk is

Convenient, social, and cozy. It feels easy, like a party, and like home.

What neuk isn’t

Complicated, restrictive, or a big commitment.

The name

Nook, a corner that offers seclusion, plus 아늑한 (aneukhan), Korean for cozy. The k is drawn as a corner: your own nook in the city.

Next steps

Next, the parts of the night the app doesn’t cover yet.

The booking loop works end to end. Three questions are still open, and the service would need a way to launch.

How might we…

If neuk launched, it would start in rooms that already exist

01

Rent before building

Pilot in karaoke rooms and older auditoriums during off-peak hours. Add a big screen and the neuk remote, and test demand without building anything.

02

Watch for repeat groups

The signal is the same friends booking again, not first-time curiosity.

03

Then build neuk rooms

Open purpose-built pods, lounges, and clubhouses in the neighborhoods that book the most.

Reflection

What began as a way to watch turned into a way to connect.

What began as a mobile booking concept became an exercise in connecting digital interactions with physical spaces, group dynamics, and the systems required to deliver a cohesive service.

Takeaway · Designing for People

People aren't just looking for a bigger screen or a better way to watch. They're looking for a place to experience it together.

Takeaway · Service design

A seamless digital experience depends on designing what happens before, during, and beyond each interaction.
