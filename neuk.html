Overview

Watching together has logistics. neuk is the infrastructure for those logistics.

neuk (느끼다 — Korean for "to feel") is a digital-physical service system that operationalizes the shared media ritual: groups book curated viewing environments, and neuk handles all the coordination seams — scheduling, content decisions, RSVPs, food, and post-session memories. The app and the physical space are designed as one integrated experience, not separate products.

System at a glance

Inputs Group size, availability signals, content preferences, location, budget
Intelligence Venue matching, content shortlisting, group voting aggregation, and session coordination — reducing the "who does the work" problem
Outputs Confirmed booking, curated session experience, and post-event memory artifact shared to the group
Feedback loop Session ratings, content selections, and attendance patterns refine venue and content suggestions for returning groups
neuk — platform overview

Problem

The coordination cost of watching together has become higher than the ritual itself.

Streaming fragmentation splits your group across five services. Scheduling requires a 40-message thread. Most urban living spaces aren't built for groups. The person who cares most ends up doing all the work — planning, booking, ordering, deciding. The social ritual of gathering to watch something meaningful has been eroded not by lack of desire, but by a coordination infrastructure that never scaled with the content ecosystem.

Coordination cost

Scheduling, content decisions, and logistics all fall on the one person most motivated to make it happen — creating a recurring leader burden

Space problem

Urban living arrangements aren't designed for group viewing — small apartments, bad acoustics, screens too small for 6+ people

Decision fatigue

Streaming sprawl means agreeing on what to watch has become its own coordination problem, often consuming as much time as the viewing itself

Solution

neuk brings the infrastructure. You bring the people.

Users browse and book curated venues — from intimate 6-person setups to cinema-scale environments — then invite their group through the app. neuk handles content curation and group voting, food coordination, and post-session highlights. The app and physical space are a single integrated experience: the app's state tracks the physical session in real time, and the venue is configured based on confirmed booking data.

The service principle: every friction point that stops a group from watching together should have a corresponding feature that removes it.

Core Flows

From idea to memory — four service touchpoints.

Discover + book a space

A map-based browse shows nearby neuk venues with capacity, vibe tags, available time slots, and AV specs. Booking with a saved group takes under 60 seconds. The venue confirms setup requirements from booking data — no manual configuration call needed.

Space discovery — map + filter view

Group content voting

Once booked, the host sends a content shortlist and the group votes asynchronously. The winning pick is confirmed to the venue automatically for setup. No 11th-hour argument. No one person deciding for the group.

Session memory

After the event, neuk assembles a shareable recap — timestamped moments, reactions, photos from the session — that lives in the group's history. The memory artifact becomes the social record of the ritual, not just a receipt.

Post-session memory card

Brand

Design that feels like a late-night screening.

neuk's visual identity leans into the warmth and intimacy of shared viewing — deep navy backgrounds, a wordmark with a slash motif referencing both play buttons and the Korean character 刀, and a type system balancing editorial weight with approachability. The brand doesn't look like a booking platform; it looks like the experience it's enabling.

Brand system — type, color, mark

Reflection

What I'd push further.

Service design requires designing the invisible coordination seams.

The hardest parts of neuk weren't the screens — they were the handoff moments between digital and physical. Who configures the room? What happens when a group shows up 20 minutes late? What's the escalation path when the AV fails? Designing those moments required a service blueprint that ran beneath the product flows — and was just as critical to the experience.

Cultural specificity is a product differentiator.

Naming the product in Korean, designing for the specific social dynamics of group watching culture, and leaning into a late-night cinematic aesthetic all came from taking the cultural context seriously — rather than building a generic venue booking product. Specificity is a competitive moat, not a limitation.
