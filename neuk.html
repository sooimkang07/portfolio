Overview

A platform for shared media rituals

neuk (느끼다 — Korean for "to feel") is a spatial and digital infrastructure platform that operationalizes the ritual of watching together. It lets groups book curated viewing environments—physical spaces equipped with AV setups, food, and atmosphere—and handles all the social coordination: scheduling, content decisions, RSVPs, and post-event memories.

neuk — platform overview

Problem

Watching together has become harder than watching alone.

Streaming fragmentation means your group is across five services. Coordinating a time requires a 40-message thread. Someone's apartment is too small; someone else doesn't have the right setup. The social ritual of gathering to watch something meaningful has been eroded by logistics that were never this hard when everyone watched the same three channels.

Coordination cost

Scheduling, content decisions, and logistics all fall on whoever cares most

Space problem

Most urban living spaces aren't built for group viewing experiences

Fragmentation

Streaming sprawl means agreeing on what to watch is harder than ever

Solution

neuk brings the infrastructure. You bring the people.

Users browse and book curated venues—from intimate living-room-scale setups to cinema-style spaces—then invite their group through the app. neuk handles content curation suggestions, group voting, food ordering, and post-session highlights. The app and physical space are designed as one integrated experience.

Core Flows

From idea to memory in four steps

Discover + book a space

A map-based browse experience shows nearby neuk venues with capacity, vibe tags, and available time slots. Booking takes under 60 seconds with a saved group.

Space discovery — map + filter view

Group content voting

Once a session is booked, the host sends a content shortlist and the group votes. The winning pick is confirmed to the venue for setup. No more 11th-hour arguments.

Session highlights

After the event, neuk assembles a shareable recap—timestamps of memorable moments, reactions, photos taken during the session—that lives in your group's history.

Post-session memory card

Brand

Design that feels like a late-night screening

neuk's visual identity leans into the warmth and intimacy of shared viewing—deep navy backgrounds, a wordmark with the slash motif referencing both play buttons and the Korean character 刀, and a type system that balances editorial weight with approachability.

Brand system — type, color, mark

Reflection

What I learned

Service design requires designing the invisible parts.

The hardest parts of neuk weren't the screens—they were the coordination seams between digital and physical. Who sets up the room? What happens if a group shows up late? Designing for those moments was as important as designing the app.

Cultural context shapes product DNA.

Naming the product in Korean, designing for the specific rhythm of group watching culture, and leaning into the late-night aesthetic all came from taking the cultural context seriously rather than designing a generic "venue booking" product.
