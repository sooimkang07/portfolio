neuk · Product Design · 2026

Designing coordination, not a booking app

Role
Lead Product Designer
Timeline
6 weeks
Scope
Service design · Strategy · Mobile
Outcome
End-to-end service: discovery to in-session control

01 / Overview

Watching together is valuable. Coordinating it kills plans.

I reframed shared viewing as a coordination problem, not a content problem. The gap isn't what to watch; it's where, and getting everyone there.

The coordination system

Inputs Group size · location · date · content type · room availability
Product Discover rooms → book → invite group → QR check-in → in-session remote + ordering
Outputs Confirmed group session · QR ticket · room control · booking history
Loop Post-session history and favorites support repeat ritual formation

Three forces breaking shared viewing

Streaming fragmentation

Content is split across services, so choosing what to watch means cross-platform negotiation before the event.

Coordination starts before anyone opens the app.

Urban living constraints

City apartments can't host the group experience people want. Theaters are inflexible; bars are loud.

There's no middle option between home and theater.

Group chat as logistics tool

Scheduling, splitting costs, and confirming attendance in a group chat is enough friction to cancel plans.

The coordination kills the plan.

The key move: reframing "book a room" as "reduce the coordination cost of gathering."

02 / Solution

One service that turns group intent into a confirmed session.

Three layers (user, venue, session) designed as one connected system.

Three interdependent layers

User layer

Discover, plan, commit. Search by location, date, capacity, and AV; invite and ticket the group in one flow.

Venue layer

Rooms are product surfaces. Availability, seating, and AV capabilities are first-class filters, not fine print.

Session layer

The phone runs the room. QR check-in unlocks playback, volume, lighting, and ordering in the same app.

03 / Core Flows

Four moments that turn a messy plan into a session.

Discovery, commitment, room activation, and return: the minimum proof of a complete service loop.

Discover: match room to group

Filter by date, group size, AV quality, and price. Screen size, seating, and streaming support surface as features, not buried specs.

Book: intent becomes commitment

Room detail to payment with a clear cost breakdown, ending in a QR ticket that's both receipt and room key.

Enter: phone becomes the room

Scanning the on-screen QR pairs the phone and unlocks the remote. The product doesn't end at the door.

Return: build the repeat ritual

Bookings sorted into active, upcoming, and past. Favorites and saved preferences speed rebooking, because the value is repeat rituals.

04 / System

neuk is a coordination layer, not a booking screen.

Designing any layer in isolation breaks the others. It only works when user, venue, and session connect end to end.

Reduce discovery friction

Surface the room attributes that drive group decisions, with availability inline to cut planning back-and-forth.

Collapse the commitment step

Browse to confirmed session in one flow. Payment, tickets, and group logistics together, with no handoff to another tool.

Extend the product into the room

QR pairing makes the phone the room's control layer, connecting booking data, room state, and in-session actions.

Support the return loop

History, favorites, and saved preferences make the next visit easier.

The value is continuity: one system replacing group chats, multiple apps, and in-person negotiation.

05 / Research

People don't want bigger screens. They want a middle ground.

Interviews kept surfacing the same gap: not content, but environment. Less overhead than hosting, more intimacy than a theater.

Environment over content

Home intimacy, theater quality, no hosting burden.

Coordination as cancellation

Group chats are a poor planning tool. Logistics friction cancels plans that would otherwise happen.

Ritual, not novelty

Sports, premieres, and weekly drops are already rituals. The product must support repetition, not novelty.

Category gap

Theaters are inflexible, bars are loud, and booking tools ignore media rituals. The category didn't exist.

06 / Design Decisions

Three scope calls that turned a concept into a service.

Each came from a specific research signal, and none were obvious at the start.

Physical gathering, not remote viewing

The gap was environment, not content access. An early watch-party prototype proved the weaker path, so the MVP centered on bookable private rooms.

Cut: remote watch parties

Another fragmented tool that solved neither space nor coordination.

Kept: bookable private rooms

Solves the environment gap and coordination overhead in one surface.

Coordination workflow, not a booking screen

The hard part is before playback: agreeing where, when, and who. Discovery through room access had to be one system.

Single flow

Discovery, payment, group ticketing, and QR access in one linear path. No app switching, no spreadsheets.

Persistent booking state

My Bookings holds upcoming, active, and past sessions, so the group never reconstructs context from messages.

Phone as the room interface

QR pairing extended neuk from booking app to service infrastructure. The remote closes the loop between digital booking and physical delivery.

07 / Reflection

Booking was the obvious scope. Coordination was the real one.

The biggest lesson was where to draw the system boundary, not what to put on screen.

Design every layer at once

Every screen decision rippled into the physical service. Designing UI alone would have produced a booking screen, not a product.

Killing a direction clarified the product

Cutting the watch-party path early, backed by consistent research, made the rest of the product sharper.
