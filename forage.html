The problem

For designers, the world is an asset library you can’t “Save As.”

Designers spot usable moments everywhere: a color on a storefront, a typeface on an awning, a motion in a crowd. Inspiration hits mid-movement, too fast to open an app, so it’s gone before it becomes material.

Research insights

Designers are stuck between finding inspiration and actually using it.

In design communities, the same complaints came up: feeds that won’t let you keep your place, and saves you can’t explain later. We track our steps, heart rate, and sleep. We still can’t track inspiration.

Defining the opportunity

“Capture anything” was too broad. Four asset types made it teachable and buildable.

Limiting captures to images, color, typography, and motion made the system coherent. Capture is also limited on purpose: no faces, no sensitive places, and every signal is visible.

Design exploration

Capture alone isn’t the story; reuse is.

The full loop: capture, interpretation, retrieval, reuse. I led the AI product architecture: how a gesture becomes a typed capture, and how the archive pulls out the palette, typeface, or motion so you can use it later.

Test & iterate

From a gadget to jewelry.

The first forms looked like devices, easy to leave at home. We modeled the ring in Rhino 3D to sit on the hand like jewelry, then tested colorways and fit across hands.

Final product

Point, gesture, and it’s in your library, sorted by what it is.

A ring captures in the moment. AI sorts each capture into an image, a color, a typeface, or a motion, and the archive keeps it findable by type, newest or earliest.

Impact

An archive of what you noticed, not what you scrolled past.

Built in 72 hours at FigBuild 2026 by four designers, with a working prototype. The goal: less cognitive load, inspiration kept, and an archive that’s yours.

See it on Devpost ↗

Reflection

Paused, but the pipeline is the part worth keeping.

The ring isn’t the strongest part. Finding the break between noticing and reuse, and designing a pipeline across it, is.

Success would be measured by whether designers pull past captures into real work, not by how often they save.

Takeaway

We can’t control when inspiration strikes, but AI and wearables can make capturing it as intuitive as one of our five senses.

Let’s make
something
worth using.
