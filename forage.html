FigBuild challenge

Design a tool for one of the human senses, in one weekend.

“Design a tool that tracks, measures, visualizes or quantifies an aspect of human sensory experience. Within the tool, provide the ability to detect, enhance, or manipulate those same sensory inputs.”
  • 72 hoursFriday, March 6 to Monday, March 9
  • 2–4 per team5,686 students registered

Figma’s 72-hour student design challenge asked teams to detect, enhance, or manipulate sensory inputs. We explored inspiration as a form of perception: how designers notice, capture, and reuse the world around them.

Problem

For designers, the world is an asset library you can’t “Save As.”

Designers find inspiration in everyday details, from colors and forms to motion and typography. But capturing these moments interrupts the experience, leaving references scattered across camera rolls and folders, disconnected from their context and difficult to retrieve.

Opportunity

How might we make capturing inspiration as effortless as noticing it, while keeping it useful for future creative work?

Solution

From observation to reusable asset, in one gesture.

Forage combines a gesture-controlled ring with an AI-powered library. Designers outline something that catches their attention, select what to capture through a gesture, and let AI transform it into an organized design asset.

Spot

Capture inspiration without reaching for a phone.

Gather

Use distinct gestures to capture images, colors, typography, or motion.

Create

Core Experience

Four ways to capture inspiration. One consistent interaction.

The wearable turns everyday observations into four types of creative assets. Each gesture corresponds to a distinct output in the digital library.

Wearable

A capture tool designed to be worn, not carried.

The ring sits on whichever finger you point with. A sculptural form and a range of finishes make it something you’d wear anyway, with capture always close at hand.

Gestures

One interaction system. Four types of creative assets.

A shared activation and selection gesture keeps the interaction consistent, while four distinct gestures determine what gets captured.

Activate

Double tap to activate.

The ring remains inactive by default, with capture initiated only by a double tap on its side, ensuring nothing is recorded until you choose to.

Image capture

Tap once for images.

Outline a visual reference and tap to save the outlined image to the Field. Each image capture downloads as a PNG or JPG.

PNG · JPG

Typography capture

Two-finger drag for typography.

Drag two fingers across lettering to extract text and identify the font or its closest publicly accessible match. The proposed experience would name the match and offer an OTF or TTF download when publicly available for download under its license.

OTF · TTF

Color capture

Two-finger tap for colors.

A two-finger tap samples a selected color and saves it to the Field. Copy it as a HEX code to drop straight into a design file.

HEX

Motion capture

Hold and drag for motion.

Hold and drag to capture a short movement. Download it as an MP4 or GIF, with its timing curve as JSON for motion design.

MP4 · GIF · Curve JSON

Digital Platform

A creative library built around what you collect.

The Field stores every capture, Boards organize them into projects, and Explore connects designers through shared inspiration.

Field

Find what you captured.

Browse captures chronologically, filter by asset type, and open individual assets to access their details.

Boards

Organize inspiration around your projects.

Save references into custom boards by project, theme, or creative direction, directly from the Field or asset details.

Explore

Discover inspiration beyond your own collection.

Browse public boards, follow designers, and save shared references into your own library.

Profile & Settings

Personalize your creative workspace.

Manage saved boards, followed designers, export preferences, visibility, and ring settings.

Research

From a familiar frustration to a wearable interaction.

Research helped us decide what to preserve, who to design for, and where capturing should happen.

Validating the problem

Inspiration gets saved, then forgotten.

With 72 hours to develop the concept, we synthesized recurring frustrations from design-community discussions about fragmented storage, lost context, and difficulty retrieving saved references.

Target user

Chia finds inspiration in everyday details.

Chia represents designers who collect typography, colors, textures, and movement throughout their day but struggle to reconnect those references to future projects.

Concept

The Invisible Sense

We interpreted inspiration as an invisible sense: the ability to recognize creative potential in everyday surroundings. Rather than proposing a new biological sense, we designed a physical interaction to capture that moment of recognition, drawing on research on creative inspiration and Carnegie Mellon design research.

Wearable landscape

Wearability shaped the form factor.

We explored jewelry and wearable references to design a discreet, accessible ring that appeals to designers’ instinct to create with their hands, using tactile gestures to make interactions feel natural rather than technological.

Build

Translating a wearable concept into a cohesive product system

We developed the physical and digital experiences in parallel, refining how the ring is worn, how gestures are distinguished, and how captures become accessible in the library.

Wearable rendering

Designing for everyday wear.

Using Rhino 3D, we refined the ring’s silhouette, placement, and finishes that suit different preferences to create a jewelry-inspired form that accommodates the proposed hardware without restricting finger movement.

Gesture fine-tuning

One shared gesture, four distinct actions.

We explored hand poses and gesture combinations to compare ease of movement and visual distinction. Using a shared outline gesture followed by an asset-specific action reduced the number of interactions designers would need to learn.

Digital product

From a generic dashboard to a capture-first experience.

I used Figma Make to quickly iterate a working prototype. The final structure prioritizes saved captures while separating three distinct workflows: collecting in the Field, organizing through Boards, and discovering through Explore.

System Design

One capture moves through hardware, intelligence, and software.

The ring notices and selects, AI turns the selection into a usable asset, and the library keeps it findable. The ring and the AI pipeline are proposed; the web prototype shows the library.

Wearable hardware concept

Every part of the ring supports one moment of the gesture.

A micro-camera captures visual input, motion sensors track spatial selection, gesture detection identifies capture types, and haptic feedback confirms actions without requiring a screen.

AI creative intelligence

Extract what designers can actually reuse.

Rather than saving only raw recordings, the proposed AI pipeline processes each capture according to its asset type, extracting information suited to a specific creative task. Privacy was part of the spec: no identifiable faces, capture turns off in sensitive places, and every record stays visible and editable.

  • ImageIsolate the selected subject and generate a reusable PNG.
  • TypographyExtract text through OCR and identify matching typefaces.
  • ColorSample selected colors and generate HEX values.
  • MotionPreserve captured movement as a clip, loop, and timing curve.

Branding

An identity inspired by collecting and creating.

Kinza and Lucy led the visual identity, developing a tactile, hand-drawn direction that connects the wearable and digital product. A sketched bloom became the central brand symbol, supported by an expressive five-color palette and playful physical applications.

Reflection

The connection between interactions is what defines the experience.

Designing Forage meant carrying one intention from a hand movement to a reusable asset. The challenge was making that continuity visible within the time we had.

Takeaway 01 · User behavior

A natural interaction must fit the moment it serves. Capturing inspiration should feel effortless, but its value comes from making it reusable.

Takeaway 02 · Designing with AI

A shared vocabulary made an ambitious system demonstrable in 72 hours. Figma Make accelerated the software, while clear boundaries kept the ring concept, simulated outputs, and working flows honest.
