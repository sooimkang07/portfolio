Smart Bundles is an Amazon Fresh concept that helps households reduce food waste through customizable ingredient bundles and personalized quantity recommendations.

Design challenge

Upgrade an existing digital ecosystem for the climate, in 24 hours.

“Pick an existing digital ecosystem and design a climate-positive upgrade that fits inside it.”
  • 24 hoursOne design sprint, concept to prototype
  • EcosystemAmazon Fresh, chosen to explore household food waste
  • ClassMethods & Practices, Parsons School of Design

Problem

Grocery apps know what shoppers buy, but not what they actually eat. Fresh loses sight of the food at the door.

Amazon Fresh makes grocery shopping convenient, but standard package sizes rarely reflect what households actually consume, and when plans change or food goes uneaten, those patterns never inform the next order.

Opportunity

How might we help households buy closer to what they'll actually eat, without requiring them to plan meals or change how they shop?

Solution

Right-sized grocery bundles that get better with every order.

Smart Bundles introduces customizable ingredient combinations into Amazon Fresh, with suggested quantities tailored to household needs. After shopping, an optional check-in captures whether those amounts were right, helping inform future recommendations without requiring meal planning or a subscription.

Core flows

Four touchpoints connect shopping behavior to smarter future orders.

Track

Correct

Share what was too much or too little to improve future bundle recommendations.

Research

The shoppers wasting the most food are often the least likely to plan ahead.

Key demographics

Design for the shopper who doesn’t plan.

A study of ~1,000 U.S. households identified "young wasters" as the highest-waste segment, despite representing just 13% of respondents, revealing a need for portion control without additional planning.

Past meal-kit solution

Amazon tried meal kits. A rigid, costly model sank them, not a lack of demand.

Pre-portioned ingredients reduced guesswork, but fixed recipes and operational challenges limited the model; Smart Bundles retains portioning without requiring prescribed meals.

Why it matters, and why now

Household food waste is costly, while online grocery infrastructure continues to expand.

Households are the largest source of food waste in the U.S. Meanwhile online grocery and meal kits are both growing, and Amazon is scaling same-day grocery to more U.S. cities. The need is there, and so is the delivery network.

Competitive analysis

Meal kits offer portion control; grocery apps offer flexibility. Neither consistently delivers both.

Smart Bundles combines customizable portions with familiar grocery shopping, without fixed recipes or subscriptions.

Iterations

I moved away from meal planning to make waste reduction the default.

I prototyped meal planning, subscriptions, and customizable bundles before choosing the approach that required the least change to existing shopping habits.

Directions

Three approaches, each with a different tradeoff

Design decisions

Make recommendations adaptable without taking control away from shoppers.

01

Recommendations remain editable.

Suggested quantities provide a starting point, while shoppers retain control over their purchases.

02

Feedback remains optional.

A quick check-in captures consumption patterns without requiring ongoing tracking.

03

Future recommendations remain adjustable.

Past feedback informs new suggestions without restricting future purchases.

System design

The next order starts with what happened to the last one.

Rather than treating every grocery order as an isolated transaction, Smart Bundles closes the gap between purchasing and consumption by using optional feedback to inform future quantity recommendations.

Ecosystem map

Changing what Fresh recommends, not how customers shop

Bundles change what’s suggested, how much arrives, and what Fresh learns after delivery, creating a learning loop without changing the disposal process.

System impact

One right-sized order pays off four ways.

Next steps

Validate whether smarter quantities actually reduce food waste.

Test with real households.

Measure overbuying and refine portion recommendations using actual purchase and consumption feedback.

Extend across Amazon's ecosystem.

Explore applying quantity recommendations to Whole Foods and Subscribe & Save.

Evaluate long-term impact.

Track repeat usage, food waste reduction, and sustained changes in shopping behavior.

Reflection

A sustainable choice only works if it’s the easy one.
