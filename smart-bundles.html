Reducing food waste through smarter defaults

Overview

One-third of all food purchased online is never eaten.

Amazon Fresh and Whole Foods Market process millions of grocery orders weekly. Yet the digital cart experience hasn't been redesigned to account for one of the biggest behavioral challenges in food retail: over-purchasing. Smart Bundles reimagines the cart as an active participant in reducing waste—not just a passive holding area for items before checkout.

Smart Bundles — overview

Problem

The cart is passive. Waste is active.

Current grocery cart UX is optimized for order completion, not order quality. Quantity defaults are set for the average household, not the individual. There's no signal about portion alignment, freshness windows, or ingredient overlap. The result: well-intentioned shoppers routinely over-buy perishables.

Behavioral Gap

Default quantities don't account for household size or consumption patterns

System Gap

No cross-item intelligence—buying spinach twice without flagging overlap

Trust Gap

No visibility into freshness windows at point-of-decision

Opportunity

Adaptive portioning as Amazon's new differentiator.

Amazon has unparalleled purchase history data. Smart Bundles uses that data to suggest right-sized quantities, flag redundant purchases, and surface recipes that use ingredients already in the cart—turning the cart from a transaction screen into a planning partner.

Opportunity framing — systems map

Solution

Smart Bundles: three behavioral interventions baked into the cart

1. Adaptive Portions

AI-suggested quantities based on your household size and purchase history, with a simple explanation of why.

2. Bundle Suggestions

Surface recipe pairings that use perishables already in your cart—encouraging complete-use purchases.

3. Freshness Flags

Inline shelf-life indicators on perishables, with a gentle nudge if your cart exceeds your estimated consumption window.

Core Flows

How Smart Bundles works in practice

Portion suggestion in the cart

When a perishable is added, an inline card surfaces a portion suggestion with a brief rationale. The user can accept, adjust, or dismiss—no friction for users who know what they want.

Cart — portion suggestion card

Bundle recipe surface

At the bottom of the cart, a carousel shows two or three recipes that use ingredients already present—with one-tap addition of any missing items at the suggested quantity.

Design Decisions

Defaults over friction

The most important design principle here was never to interrupt the checkout flow. Every Smart Bundles intervention is dismissible in one tap. The goal is to make the sustainable choice the default, not the forced choice.

Reflection

What I learned

Behavioral design needs data infrastructure.

Smart Bundles only works if Amazon already knows your household patterns. Designing for a data-rich context required thinking about how to earn—and maintain—that trust through transparency.

Systemic problems need systemic solutions.

Solving food waste through one-off UI nudges wouldn't move the needle. The power of Smart Bundles is that all three interventions reinforce each other, creating a compounding effect on shopping behavior over time.
