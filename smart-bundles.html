Amazon Fresh concept · 24-hour sprint · 2026

Smart Bundles is an Amazon Fresh concept that helps households buy closer to what they actually eat through customizable ingredient bundles and smarter quantity recommendations.

Design challenge

Upgrade an existing digital ecosystem for the climate, in 24 hours.

A 24-hour design sprint for my Methods & Practices class at Parsons. The prompt:

“Pick an existing digital ecosystem and design a climate-positive upgrade that fits inside it.”
  • 24 hoursOne design sprint, concept to prototype
  • EcosystemAmazon Fresh, chosen to explore household food waste
  • ClassMethods & Practices, Parsons School of Design

Problem

Grocery apps know what shoppers buy, but not what they actually eat.

Amazon Fresh makes purchasing groceries convenient, but standard package sizes don't always match how much a household needs. When plans change or food goes uneaten, that information never informs the next order. The result is a recurring disconnect between what customers buy and what they consume.

Where waste accumulates today

Fresh loses sight of the food at the door.

Opportunity

How might we help households buy closer to what they'll actually eat, without requiring them to plan meals or change how they shop?

Solution

Right-sized grocery bundles that get better with every order.

Smart Bundles introduces customizable ingredient combinations into Amazon Fresh, with suggested quantities tailored to household needs. After shopping, an optional check-in captures whether those amounts were right, helping inform future recommendations without requiring meal planning or a subscription.

Core flows

Four touchpoints connect shopping behavior to smarter future orders.

Smart Bundles builds on the existing Amazon Fresh journey, introducing quantity recommendations and a lightweight feedback loop without requiring customers to adopt a new shopping routine.

Touchpoint 01

Discover bundles

Find ingredient combinations based on familiar shopping behavior, then open a bundle to see what’s included.

Touchpoint 02

Make it your own

Adjust portions and quantities before adding the bundle to the cart. Recommendations stay editable.

Touchpoint 03

Follow the delivery

Keep the familiar delivery experience, with the right-sized order on its way.

Touchpoint 04

Help the next order fit

Give optional feedback on what was too much or too little to inform future bundle sizes.

Research

The smallest group wastes the most.

So the fix can’t depend on planning ahead.

Key demographics

Design for the shopper who doesn’t plan.

A national study of ~1,000 U.S. households sorts shoppers into three groups. The smallest, young wasters, throws out the most food, and what would help them most (portion control, storage, meal planning) all takes effort they don’t spend.

Past meal-kit solution

Amazon tried meal kits. A rigid, costly model sank them, not a lack of demand.

Amazon Meal Kits were pre-portioned but tied to set recipes, and they ran into high food waste, thin margins, and low retention. Shoppers still asked where they went. I kept the portioning and dropped the recipe lock-in.

Why it matters, and why now

Households waste the most food, and the demand for help is growing.

Households are the largest source of food waste in the U.S. Meanwhile online grocery and meal kits are both growing, and Amazon is scaling same-day grocery to more U.S. cities. The need is there, and so is the delivery network.

Competitive analysis

No one is both flexible and low-waste.

Meal kits portion ingredients but lock people into recipes. Grocery apps are flexible but sell default pack sizes that over-buy. Smart Bundles keeps regular grocery shopping and changes the default amount.

Iterations

I moved away from meal planning to make waste reduction the default.

I explored meal plans, subscriptions, and customizable bundles through rapid Figma Make prototypes. While structured plans could help customers manage portions, they also required the planning behavior my research suggested was least common among high-waste shoppers. I narrowed the concept to flexible ingredient bundles within the existing Fresh journey, reducing the need for customers to change their shopping habits.

Directions considered

From broad explorations to one adaptable shopping experience.

System design

The next order starts with what happened to the last one.

Smart Bundles connects product discovery, quantity selection, and post-purchase feedback within the existing Amazon Fresh journey. Rather than treating every grocery order as an isolated transaction, the concept uses customer feedback to inform future quantity recommendations.

Ecosystem map

Change the defaults, then close the loop.

Bundles change what’s suggested, how much arrives, and what Fresh learns after delivery. Disposal stays as it is; the goal is less to dispose of.

Design decisions

Make recommendations adaptable without taking control away from shoppers.

01

Recommendations remain editable.

Suggested quantities provide a starting point, but customers retain control over their purchases. This accommodates different appetites, household sizes, and changing plans.

02

Feedback remains optional.

A lightweight check-in creates an opportunity to learn from previous purchases without turning grocery shopping into another tracking task.

03

Future recommendations remain adjustable.

Customer feedback can inform subsequent quantity suggestions, but a previous response should not permanently determine what someone can buy.

System impact

One right-sized order pays off four ways.

These are the effects a pilot would need to prove, not measured results.

Next steps

Validate whether better quantity recommendations actually reduce waste.

A pilot would show whether bundles cut over-buying without making checkout harder. These are the targets I’d hold it to.

01 · Optimize

Validate in real households.

Test whether bundling reduces over-buying, then refine the default portions with real usage data.

02 · Expand

Carry it across Amazon.

Bring portion intelligence to Whole Foods and Subscribe & Save as an embedded default.

03 · Measure

Look for lasting change.

Track long-term waste reduction, habit formation, and sustainability impact.

Reflection

A sustainable choice only works if it’s the easy one.

Takeaway · Users

People don’t waste food because they don’t care. Plans change, so the default has to absorb that instead of asking them to plan better.

Takeaway · Designing with AI

AI made forty directions cheap in a day. Choosing the one behavior worth designing for was still my job.
