Instagram · Product design · Concept · 2025

Instagram Lists is a concept that lets people group who they follow and choose which group their feed shows first.

Role
Solo product designer
Timeline
7 weeks
Nov–Dec 2025
Platform
Instagram
iOS concept
Scope
Research
Systems design
Hi-fi prototype

Problem

Everyone you follow competes in one feed.

Opportunity

How might we give users control over what their feed prioritizes in each moment without sacrificing discovery?

Solution

Choose who your feed is for. Instagram ranks inside that choice.

People pick a list, and Home, Stories, and Following all follow it. Ranking still works, just on the right pool of accounts.

Core flows

Lists live inside browsing, not beside it.

01 · Defaults

Users start with Default Lists.

02 · Extend

Users can then extend this system into their own relationships.

03 · Create

Creation happens where intent emerges.

04 · Edit

Editing happens where intent already exists.

Research

Algorithms can predict relevance, not intent.

Takeaway 01 · Platform audits & competitive analysis

Instagram has discovery and friends, but no way to choose between them.

I audited Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and Snapchat. Instagram covers algorithmic discovery and friend-first interaction, but viewing modes are limited and there’s no intentional content filtering. Intentional viewing was the open space.

Takeaway 02 · User interviews & affinity mapping

People already sort who they follow. Instagram mixes it all back together.

Think-aloud sessions with five active users, synthesized in an affinity map. People scrolled for a long time without engaging, and described friends, school, creators, and brands as separate groups in their heads.

Explorations

Three directions, tested with 10 people. Only one fixed the root problem.

Navigation and profile ideas improved control, not priority. Only organizing who you follow changed what the ranking receives.

System thinking

A new feed model: Context + Ranking

Instagram doesn’t stop ranking. People set the context first, and ranking works inside it.

System mapping

Designed as a system,
not just a feature.

I prioritized with MoSCoW, mapped every surface Lists touches, and traced the journeys to create, filter, and edit a list, so one choice holds across Home, Stories, profiles, and Following.

Design decisions

Native to Instagram, and private by default.

Decision 01 · System fit

No new visual language.

I started from Instagram’s own badges, icons, and buttons, tested list markers across stories, profiles, and menus, and kept only what read as native.

Decision 02 · Privacy

Lists only change what you see.

Sorting people is personal, so a list is visible only to the person who made it. No one is notified when they’re added, lists never show on anyone’s profile, and the people on a list see nothing different. The list markers on stories and profiles are shown only to you.

Impact & next steps

If validated, Lists could help…

  1. Users

    Focus on what matters most in each moment.

  2. Creators

    Reach the right audiences.

  3. Instagram

    Drive high-intent engagement without losing discovery.

How I’d measure it

An A/B test against today’s feed, with discovery as the guardrail.

Lists is a concept, so I’d set the measures before claiming results. The baseline comes from the research: people scrolled for a long time without engaging.

  1. 01

    Adoption

    Share of active users who switch to a list at least once a week.

  2. 02

    Intent

    Likes, comments, and replies per session on accounts in the active list, compared with the default feed.

  3. 03

    Guardrail: discovery

    Suggested posts seen and followed per session, so choosing a list never quietly cuts discovery.

Next steps

Where Lists goes next.

  1. 01

    Multiple lists

    Browse more than one list at once, like Friends and Food together, without building a new one.

  2. 02

    AI-generated lists

    Suggest lists from who people already interact with, so setup takes a tap instead of sorting. Suggestions stay private and opt-in, and nothing is sorted until the user confirms it.

  3. 03

    Refine ad relevancy

    Use the active list as a signal, so ads match what someone chose to browse right now.

  4. 04

    Lists on Instagram Plus

    Plus already lets members share a story with multiple audiences. Lists is the viewing side of the same idea: default lists stay free, and Plus members get unlimited custom lists and multiple lists at once.

Reflection

When users can control each session, they stay focused on and return to what matters most to them.

I presented this case study to Ian Goode, former Head of Meta Design, and a panel with Darren Chin and my professors Sally Chung, Dana Jefferson, and Lauren Schlanger. In the mock interview, I was marked Advanced.
