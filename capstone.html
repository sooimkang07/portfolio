Overview

This case study is in progress.

My senior capstone is a year-long product design project in collaboration with a community partner. The full documentation — research, process, outcomes — will be published here upon completion in May 2026. What follows is an early snapshot of the problem space and design direction.

Capstone — coming May 2026

Problem

The problem space: civic information access

Local government information is fragmented across dozens of websites, PDFs, and phone trees — making it nearly impossible for residents, especially non-English speakers and elderly populations, to find what they need when they need it. Our capstone team is partnering with a city agency to understand and redesign the resident information experience from the ground up.

Who's affected

Residents navigating permits, benefits, services, and community programs — especially those with lower digital literacy

The core friction

Information exists but is buried, inconsistently formatted, and never organized around resident mental models

The opportunity

A resident-facing interface that surfaces the right information at the right moment, in plain language, across devices

Research

Understanding how residents actually find information

We conducted 14 contextual interviews with residents across three city neighborhoods, focusing on recent moments when they had to navigate a city service. We also audited 47 city-facing web pages for readability, navigation structure, and mobile accessibility.

Research — interview synthesis map

Key finding: residents trust people over pages

Across every interview, residents described going to a neighbor, a community organizer, or a family member before ever attempting to find information on a city website. The informal network was faster and more reliable than the official one. This shaped our entire design direction.

Direction

Designing for trust before usability

Our hypothesis: no amount of information architecture will solve the problem if residents don't trust the source. The design challenge became how to build a digital interface that earns trust the way community members do — through plain language, visible accountability, and proactive communication.

Process

Where we are now

We've completed research synthesis and have landed on three potential design directions. We're currently in a structured concept development phase, running low-fi tests with community members biweekly. Final designs and outcomes will be published here in May 2026.

Reflection

In progress

This project has already surfaced something I didn't expect: the hardest design problems aren't technical — they're relational. Designing for a community partner means navigating institutional constraints, political sensitivities, and the gap between what stakeholders say they want and what residents actually need.

Full reflection coming May 2026, once we've shipped and measured impact.
