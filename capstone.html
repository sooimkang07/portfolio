01 / Overview

In progress: redesigning how residents find city services.

My senior capstone: a year-long project with a city agency. Full research, system model, and outcomes publish in May 2026. Below: the framing, research, and principles guiding the work.

System at a glance

Inputs

Resident intent (permits, benefits, services, programs), literacy level, language, device type

Intelligence

IA built around resident mental models, not agency org charts, with plain language and proactive status updates

Outputs

The right information at the right moment, in plain language, before residents have to ask

Feedback loop

Search queries, task abandonment, and community navigator flags reveal gaps to fix

Capstone — coming May 2026

02 / Problem

Civic information is organized for agencies, not for residents.

City information is scattered across websites, PDFs, and phone trees, organized by department. To find a permit or benefit, residents first have to understand how the city is organized.

Who's affected

Anyone navigating permits, benefits, services, or programs; hardest on residents with lower digital literacy, non-English speakers, and elderly people

The structural problem

Information follows agency structure, not resident mental models, so the translation burden falls entirely on residents

The design opportunity

A system organized around "what do I need to do?" instead of "which department owns this?", with proactive status updates built in

03 / Research

Residents trust people over pages. That's a signal.

We ran 14 contextual interviews across three city neighborhoods about recent city-service tasks, and audited 47 city web pages for readability, navigation, and mobile accessibility.

Research — interview synthesis map

Informal networks are the real infrastructure

Every participant went to a neighbor, organizer, or family member before a city website. That's a rational response, not a literacy gap.

Architecture is the problem, not content

The information exists, but it's buried and organized for administrators. The job is restructuring what surfaces, to whom, and when.

04 / Direction

Design for trust before usability.

Our working hypothesis: better IA won't help if residents don't trust the source. So the primitives are plain language, visible accountability, and anticipatory communication. Usability follows trust.

Design principles — trust framework

05 / Process

Testing three directions within real institutional constraints.

Research synthesis is done. Three design directions are in biweekly low-fi testing with community members. The other design problem: the gap between what agency representatives want and what residents need.

06 / Reflection

Civic design is relational and political, not technical.

So far, the hardest problems in civic tech are relational and political. Balancing resident needs against real, sometimes immovable institutional constraints is as much a design skill as wireframing.

Full reflection and outcomes coming May 2026, after we ship and measure impact with our partner.
