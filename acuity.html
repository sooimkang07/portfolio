Overview

A proactive health intelligence ecosystem built in 48 hours

Most wearable health devices are reactive—they alert you after something goes wrong. Acuity is a smart ring ecosystem designed to detect subtle physiological baseline shifts before they escalate, translating raw biometric data into tiered, actionable interventions. Built at a hackathon in 48 hours with a team of four.

Acuity ring — hardware + app overview

Problem

Wearables show you data. They don't tell you what to do with it.

Existing rings and smartwatches surface heart rate, HRV, sleep scores. But translating a "Readiness: 62" into a meaningful action requires expertise most users don't have. Acuity bridges that gap by converting biometric drift into human-readable, tiered interventions—from subtle lifestyle nudges to urgent prompts to seek care.

Gap 1

Data is presented without personal baseline context, making it hard to know what's "normal" for you

Gap 2

No tiered response system—every alert feels equally urgent (or is ignored)

Gap 3

Reactive alerts come too late in the physiological timeline to prevent escalation

Solution

Acuity: four sensor modalities, one intelligence layer

The Acuity ring monitors HRV, skin temperature, blood oxygen, and sleep stage. An on-device ML model establishes a personalized 30-day baseline, then surfaces deviations as tiered interventions: Level 1 (habit nudge), Level 2 (schedule rest), Level 3 (consult a professional).

Core Flows

How Acuity guides users through a health event

Daily briefing

Every morning, Acuity surfaces a three-metric summary—how your night compared to your baseline, with a single recommended action for the day.

Daily briefing screen

Tiered alert flow

When a metric drifts outside the user's personal threshold, Acuity triggers a tiered alert. Level 1 is a quiet in-app card; Level 3 is a persistent notification with a direct link to telehealth booking.

Trend visualization

A 30-day trend view shows how each metric has moved relative to baseline—helping users connect lifestyle choices to physiological patterns over time.

Trend view — 30-day baseline comparison

Hardware Constraints

Designing for a device that never leaves your hand

Smart rings have significant constraints: minimal screen real estate for haptic feedback, battery life under 7 days at full sensor polling, and the social context of wearing it in every setting. We designed Acuity's app to carry the full cognitive load so the ring itself only needed to do two things: sense and vibrate.

Reflection

What I learned

Health UX requires deep trust calibration.

Every design decision—how we labeled alert levels, how we worded recommendations—had to be carefully tuned to avoid either creating anxiety or encouraging users to ignore signals that mattered.

Speed of iteration is a design skill.

Forty-eight hours forces brutal prioritization. We shipped three flows instead of twelve, but made those three flows airtight. The constraint improved the product.
