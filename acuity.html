Health wearable + app · Hackathon · 2026

Acuity is an ecosystem for clarity before crisis: a bracelet that senses, AI that learns your normal, and an app that tells you when to act. Designed in a 24-hour hackathon.

Role
Product Designer
The app
Team
Sooim · app
Lucy · brand, deck
Kritika · research
Timeline
24 hours
Status
Hackathon concept

Problem

Half of people feel warning signs before cardiac arrest. Only one in five act.

Wearables already show heart rate, blood oxygen, and ECG, then leave the meaning to you. Is this serious, or just stress? Most people guess, and wait.

Solution

The bracelet senses, AI interprets, and the app tells you what to do.

Acuity learns your normal, flags sustained drift rather than one-off spikes, and turns it into one of three states. Each state comes with a plain next step.

Research

The most dangerous moment isn’t the emergency. It’s the uncertainty before it.

Kritika’s research kept landing on the same gap: people notice something is off, but can’t tell if it’s worth acting on. The people at highest risk are also the least equipped to decide.

Core flows

One question on every screen: do I need to act?

I designed the app around three moments: knowing where you stand, understanding an alert, and responding from the wrist.

States

Three states answer one question: do I need to act?

Stable, Elevated, Emergent. A yes/no alert either over-triages or misses signals, so each state gets proportionate language and one clear action.

Alert

An Elevated alert says what changed, what it means, and what to do next.

The change is measured against your baseline, not a population average, and explained in plain words. The main action is “Schedule evaluation.”

Wrist

Two buzzes, a pinch to see why, a wave to dismiss.

Most moments shouldn’t need the phone. The bracelet signals a change and lets you check or dismiss it with a gesture.

Iterations

Three disciplines in one sprint: research, brand, and the app built side by side.

Kritika’s research set the problem, Lucy built the brand and deck, and I designed the app. Working in parallel meant the app’s states, the brand’s colors, and the pitch all told the same story by hour 24.

Design decisions

We started designing for everyone, then narrowed to the people most at risk.

Broad tools had shallow impact, and they missed the people who need monitoring most, who rarely buy wearables. So Acuity reaches high-risk patients through insurers and health systems, with real care behind every alert.

Reflection

Named one of the standout concepts at the hackathon.

24 hours forced one thesis instead of a feature list. The bracelet isn’t the story. Deciding when to speak, and how urgently, is.

Takeaway

Health data is finally accurate; the value now is making it clear enough to act on before a crisis.
