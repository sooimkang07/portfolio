ACUITY · AI Health Monitoring · 24hr Challenge · 2026

Deciding when a health signal needs action

Role
Systems + Product Designer
Timeline
24 hours
Scope
Hardware concept · Mobile app · Service pathway
Focus
Escalation logic for biometric signals

01 / Overview

Wearables detect signals but never say what to do.

Patients see elevated metrics but don't know what they mean or what to do. ACUITY escalates sustained baseline deviation with proportionate urgency: nudge → schedule → urgent care.

The data-action gap: metrics without meaning

The problem

Wearables surface heart rate, blood oxygen, and ECG, then leave interpretation to the user. Is this serious, or just stress?

The opportunity

Detect sustained baseline drift, classify risk, and route users to the right care without alarming or over-triaging.

The constraint

High-risk patients are least likely to self-diagnose. Insurers and health systems reach them with real care pathways, not app notifications.

02 / Solution

The real design surface is when the system speaks.

ACUITY detects sustained deviation from a patient's baseline and routes them to the right care tier. I treated the "when do we speak?" logic as the core UX.

Biometrics → baseline → escalation logic → patient action

Personalized baseline

Learns each patient's normal from the first weeks of monitoring, replacing population averages with a personal reference point.

Deviation detection

Flags sustained patterns, not one-off spikes. Magnitude, duration, and symptom context set the escalation tier.

Tiered interventions

Tier 1 monitor → Tier 2 schedule → Tier 3 urgent. Each tier carries proportionate language, confidence signals, and a clear next step.

03 / Core Flows

Four touchpoints prove the loop from signal to care.

Baseline, alert, scheduling, and provider handoff: the minimum proof of an end-to-end care pathway.

Learn a personal baseline

The bracelet captures vitals at rest, during activity, and in sleep, so deviation is measured against this patient's normal.

Baseline learning flow

Alerts that explain the why

Sustained drift triggers an alert showing risk tier, plain-language meaning, and confidence. Tier 2's primary CTA is "Schedule evaluation"; Tier 3 escalates.

Tiered alert UI

Low-friction handoff to care

The product is only as good as follow-through. Scheduling pre-fills alert context, cuts steps, and confirms what happens next.

Appointment scheduling flow

Clinician-ready summary

Translates drift and symptom context into medical terms, so providers triage fast and patients never interpret their own charts.

Clinician summary report

04 / System

Legible escalation logic, not AI magic, earns trust.

ACUITY weighs drift magnitude, duration, and symptom context to route patients to the right tier, with high confidence and low false-alarm rates.

Deviation detector + logic gate system

Deviation detector

Compares live signals to the personal baseline, adjusting for activity, time of day, and recent stress.

Logic gate

Combines confidence thresholds, risk priors, and reported symptoms. Built for false positives: escalates only after sustained drift, in cautious language.

Care pathway

Each tier connects to real care through health systems and insurers.

05 / Design Decisions

Four tradeoffs made ACUITY credible in 24 hours.

Each one favored the system behaviors that create trust.

Personal baseline over population norms

Population thresholds miss meaningful individual drift. I chose per-patient baselines, making the system sensitive to personal change.

Three tiers over binary alerts

A binary alert either over-triages or misses signals. Three tiers allow proportionate responses: nudge, scheduled evaluation, or urgent care.

Insurer deployment over direct-to-consumer

Those who most need monitoring rarely buy wearables. Insurers and health systems reach them and plug into real care pathways.

Clinician summary over raw dashboards

Providers need context, not charts. A drift summary in medical terms enables faster triage and higher follow-through rates.

Decision framework

06 / Reflection

The 24-hour limit forced a thesis, not a gallery.

Time pressure forced systems thinking

I designed a closed loop to test one hypothesis: can drift be detected and routed to appropriate care?

Infrastructure beats novelty

The wearable isn't the story. The intervention engine is: deciding when to act, how confidently, and where to route care.
