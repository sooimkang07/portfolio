yap is an AI-powered voice messaging app that organizes recordings into topics, so friends can listen, revisit, and reply to individual thoughts without losing the flow of conversation. A progressive web app (PWA) independently designed, built, and launched in six weeks.

Role
Product designer Engineer
Timeline
6 weeks, May 2026 Graduate capstone
Platform
Progressive web app Launched May 2026 SMS now paused
Scope
Product design Systems design AI architecture Development

Problem

Friends want to catch up the way they actually talk: long, rambly, and in their own voice. But the tools we use today make us all compromise someway or another.

Opportunity

How might we make catching up asynchronously feel as natural and easy to follow as talking in person?

Solution

Long voice chats, organized by topic instead of time

yap uses AI to identify topic changes in long voice memos, turning each thought into a thread friends can listen and reply to separately, without losing the spontaneity of the original recording.

Core features

From long voice memos to ongoing conversations

CATCH UP

Listen by topic or all at once.

Each memo arrives as labeled topic cards in the group chat. Tap a topic to hear just that part, or tap play next to the mic to open Now Playing and hear everything new, topic by topic.

RESPOND

Talk freely. AI organizes the replies.

Record one rambly, uninterrupted memo. AI threads responses under existing topics and creates new ones when the conversation shifts.

STAY ORGANIZED

Pick up every conversation where you left off.

Replies stay grouped by topic, with playback starting from the first unheard response.

Join

Invite friends by text. No download required.

Sign up with your phone number, create a group, and invite friends through an SMS link that opens in their browser.

Iterations

Three directions, tested with 30 people

I prototyped three directions in Figma Make and tested them with 30 people, which informed how the final product organizes conversations, supports playback, and keeps the experience voice-first.

Build

From Figma prototype to working product in six weeks

I designed, developed, and launched yap independently using Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex. I owned the product decisions, implementation, testing, and deployment, using AI coding agents to accelerate development without compromising the intended experience.

Stack

Built for frictionless access, without an app download

I chose a progressive web app (PWA) to let friends join through SMS. Browser recording captures memos, serverless AI organizes topics, Supabase syncs conversations, and Twilio handles invitations and notifications.

AI

AI identifies topic changes. Code makes the precise cuts.

Yap matches AI-identified topic changes to word-level transcript timestamps for precise audio cuts, while distinguishing replies from new conversations. If processing fails, it retries segmentation or preserves the original memo.

Sync

Keeping conversations in sync across devices

Behind every yap is a system that keeps recordings, topics, replies, and listening progress synchronized across friends. Supabase manages this shared conversation state in real time, while Twilio sends SMS notifications so friends know when there's something new to hear.

Testing & debugging

Real conversations exposed what early prototypes couldn't.

Remote and in-person testing revealed three critical issues in AI segmentation, playback, and message delivery.

Bug 01 · AI Segmentation

The AI struggled to accurately separate topics in longer voice memos.

When AI merged unrelated topics or misidentified boundaries, I improved segmentation by matching each topic’s opening words to word-level transcript timestamps for precise playback, with sentence-based splitting as a fallback.

Bug 02 · Audio Playback

Playing any topic restarted the entire voice memo.

To fix topic playback starting from the beginning, I used transcript-aligned timestamps to play only the selected segment without splitting the original recording.

Bug 03 · Sync & Notifications

A successful recording didn’t always mean a delivered message.

To fix memos failing to reach group members or trigger SMS notifications, I corrected synchronization issues across Supabase and Twilio and verified delivery through cross-device testing.

Launch

I launched yap live at MPS Communication Design Demo Day 2026.

Visitors could sign up at joinyap.chat ↗ and exchange real voice memos from their own phones. I designed promotional materials and a feedback notebook to introduce the product and collect firsthand reactions.

Feedback

yap made an impression beyond the demo.

Following Demo Day, an attendee included yap in his tweet of standout projects, describing it as a “voice inbox for extreme yappers.”

Reflection

Building yap taught me that designing with AI means designing for its limitations, too.

Taking yap from prototype to product required defining technical constraints, testing real conversations, and resolving cross-device reliability issues beyond what AI coding agents could handle.

Takeaway 01 · User behavior

Voice-first design means preserving the spontaneity of speaking while giving listeners more control over how they catch up.

Takeaway 02 · Designing with AI

The quality of an AI-powered experience depends as much on the constraints, fallbacks, and interactions surrounding the model as on the model itself.
