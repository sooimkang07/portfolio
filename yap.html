Voice-first PWA · 0→1 · 2026

yap is a voice-first group chat where AI splits long voice memos into topics friends can reply to one by one. Designed and built as a live PWA.

Role
Solo designer
Builder
Scope
Product design
Interaction design
Build
Launch
Platform
Mobile web app (PWA)
Status
Live

Problem

A four-minute voice memo gets a one-word reply.

One memo can hold five different things. The reply usually answers one of them. Nobody scrubs back through four minutes to find the rest, so it gets buried.

Solution

AI finds the topics and leaves the voice alone.

The sender talks once. yap transcribes the memo, finds where the topic changes, and texts the group. Friends play only the new topics and reply to the one they care about.

Research

People don’t skip voice memos because they’re long. They skip them because there’s no way to answer part of one.

Length wasn’t the complaint. Replying was: there’s no way to point at one part of a memo, so everything after the first reply goes unanswered.

Core flows

Send, reply, catch up.

The sender talks once. Everyone else answers and listens topic by topic.

Send

Talk once. yap turns it into topics.

Tap the mic and talk. yap organizes the memo, and the topics appear as cards in the chat.

Reply

Reply to the part you actually have something to say about.

Each topic has an AI-written label, its own clip, and a line of what was said, so you know what it is before you play it. Your voice reply lives inside that topic.

Catch up

New topics come first, so catching up takes seconds, not minutes.

Unheard topics are marked. Instead of playing every memo start to finish, you play only what’s new.

Design decisions

I shipped a PWA with text-message invites, so friends join from a link, not an App Store.

A native iOS app meant the App Store or TestFlight, an install before anyone could reply, and extra Apple setup for push. With a PWA, a friend gets a text, taps the link, and is in the chat.

Iterations

The first version made one long group recording easier to scrub. The shipped one breaks it into topics.

The early direction was one group audio timeline with a transcript, @mentions, 1×–2× speed, and “Jump in” to speak. Speed and transcripts made it easier to scrub, but it was still one long timeline. Only topics fixed replying.

Branding

Soft, glassy, and voice-first: the record button is the brand.

A pastel gradient field, glass controls, system type, a glow for each participant, and a coral waveform. The components are built around recording and playing voice.

Launch

A voice app needs a physical presence: stickers and posters sent people to the app.

A link alone doesn’t get people talking into their phones. I printed stickers and posters that pointed people to the app.

Reflection

yap didn’t stop at a prototype: it shipped as a live app, and I presented it at the final showcase.

Friends join from a text with no install: enter your number, enter the code, and the chat opens.

Takeaway

AI can separate topics, match replies, and track what’s been heard – turning fragmented, out-of-sync voice memos into a conversation people can actually follow.
