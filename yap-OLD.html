yap · Product Design · 2026

Voice chats organized by topic, not timeline

Role
Solo designer
Timeline
2 weeks · Jun 2026
Platform
Voice chat app concept
Scope
Product design · Interaction design · Prototyping

01 / Overview

Long voice calls get lost in chronology.

Voice calls run long and span many topics, but apps file them by time. Once the feed moves on, what was said is gone.

Problem

Users can't find past points in a call. Nothing organizes long calls by subject.

Opportunity

Let people mark topics as they talk. Timestamps become searchable; calls become navigable.

How might we make voice calls navigable by topic?

02 / Solution

One tap mid-call marks a topic shift.

A tap during the call marks a topic shift. Afterward, the app builds a searchable outline.

Topics make calls searchable

After the call, jump straight to the discussion you need.

03 / Core Flows

Marking can never interrupt the conversation.

Marking had to happen inside the call: one tap, then name the topic.

Mark without pausing the call

A button in the call controls adds a marker without pausing the conversation.

The outline appears after

Participants see what was discussed, by topic. Tap any topic to jump to its timestamp.

04 / System

Topics live inside the call, not beside it.

It works because it lives in the existing call interface, not as a separate feature.

Before topics

Long calls become one unsearchable recording.

With topics

Calls become outlines you can navigate and revisit.

The value: ephemeral talk becomes reference material.

05 / Research

Voice is harder to revisit than text.

Users struggle to find moments in past calls, and often re-record audio to document decisions.

Voice is rich but unsearchable

Audio carries tone and nuance, but you can't search or skim it.

Long calls blur together

Users remember that something was said, not when.

Context gets lost

Without structure, key moments get lost.

06 / Design Decisions

Every decision protected focus on the conversation.

The key decisions minimized cognitive load and kept attention on the call, not the tool.

Structural fit

The marker lives in the call controls, reachable without breaking conversational flow.

Keep markers lightweight

One tap to mark, a quick voice label after.

Show the outline after

The outline appears when the call ends, when people are ready to use it.

Navigation fit

The outline mirrors messaging-app patterns, so navigation needs no learning.

Simple, scannable outlines

Topic name and timestamp only; tap to jump to the segment.

Allow flexible labeling

People name topics in their own words, with no fixed taxonomy.

07 / Reflection

Structure should emerge from behavior.

The best organizing systems ride on what people already do. Voice calls don't need transcription or tagging workflows, just one natural moment to mark what matters.

Timing is everything

Organize when something feels important, not after the fact.

Simplicity scales

One tap beats complex tagging, if it lives where the behavior happens.

Voice is underrated

Design often treats voice as a fallback. Structured well, it's richer than text.
